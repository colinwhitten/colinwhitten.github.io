The Disappearers

After my eyes were molested by the pretentious filth in Life of M and I was in desperate need of a palette cleanser, The Disappearers was published. It’s by a real Writer named Marlon James and it lives up to the hype and more! It was nice to leave behind the pretentiousness of Rachel Cusk’s autofiction, a style which feels like a gentrification of life experiences. I was happy to return to the dark and depraved world of Marlon James’ Jamaica. 

In Rachel Cusk’s Life of M we learn what a character is not. If you are curious what a character may be, it might be someone whose sense of self is so assured that they feel compelled to live out the exploration of their sexuality despite the risk of being openly, or quietly gay in a place as homophobic as: Jamaica in the 80s; or a place as transphobic as… everywhere, all the time! It’s brave to do this publicly, and it’s brave to do this privately! Everything about The Disappearers feels pertinent especially within the context of the current Trans Panic. Why do so many people get worked up about trans people wanting to be trans? 

There’s a thread throughout this book of a closeted gay man participating in the event which the rest of the novel is centered around: a violent homophobic attack on a group of gay men who are preparing to perform a stage play. It’s certainly sometimes the case that someone is homophobic specifically because they’re still working out their own sexuality. I think it’s too easy of an out to give these people, and it would be far too satisfying for us to point at homophobes and say that they’re hateful because they’re all secretly gay. Not all homophobes are closeted themselves. Some are hetero and just plain hateful. Why is that!

I think a lot of people are mad to see other people having a sense of self!
