Kairos, Age Gap Relationships and Bad Weather

Kairos by Jenny Erpenbeck

I’d love to skip this one. Book sucked in my opinion. Jenny Erpenbeck has got a fucked up sense of humor because why was the ideal vessel for exploring post-reunification life in Berlin a relationship between a man who is something like fifty-four and a nineteen year old girl? I understand that their relationship is a metaphor for having completely different understandings and feelings of nostalgia for the East Germany you grew up in; a sixty year old man and a teenage girl would totally experience newly reunified Berlin differently if they both experienced coming-of-age in East Berlin, but during completely different times of their lives. 


Something to think about: how would we feel about Communist architecture if the Bolshevik revolution had happened in a place with Awesome Weather? It’s easy for me to remember what I’ve been told by people who had ever visited former East Berlin because they all say the same thing: they all report that it was gray! No fucking shit! If you visited Berlin at any time other than between June and August, then it was gray. It’s a place that’s really sore on the eyes, but I wouldn’t necessarily say it’s because of some fatal flaw in affordable and efficient housing blocks.


Otherwise this is a book that is very generally about an affair which plays out during the end of an era and beginning of a new one. The State decays and ages poorly as their affair, decays and ages poorly. There is another theme that I find a little more interesting which is one that runs contrary to the sort of mainstream historical perspective of East Berlin being dull, gray, and lifeless (loveless?). More thoughts on gray as well. East Berlin was communist, had soviet architecture, and was therefore cold and lifeless. And this book centers that setting as a place where a love affair blossoms. Something to think about: I remember early last year in 2025 when we Americans were having a collective freakout about the fact that our country is now Fascist, and we can’t ignore the fact that it’s Fascist anymore: how did this happen, and what do we do!? We will have inappropriate age-gap relationships and affairs. We will fall in love in disgusting and offensive ways. We will also fall in love in appropriate ways! We’ll kind of do the same thing we were doing two years ago, or twenty years ago, or in 1990 in Berlin.
