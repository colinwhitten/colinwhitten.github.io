Vineland

  1. Vineland, by Thomas Pynchon

    Like any other Pynchon book, especially reading for the first time, you’re supposed to let yourself get lost, though this is apparently one of his most accessible works, or maybe even Pynchon-lite. Nevertheless I might not even be keen enough to get a grip on Pynchon-lite as I was still almost completely lost.

    Though it was published in 1990 it’s still extremely prescient, clearly. It’s very broadly about the free spirits of the 60s helplessly watching America march forward into the 80s as if in the blink of an eye, and contending with the fact that their project failed. What is it about this cultural moment? Why do we hear more from the people of 60s counterculture than seemingly any American generation? It’s probably one of the most run-through tropes, these geezers who smoked pot, went to Woodstock, and protested the Vietnam War… they lost; and we will never hear the end of it. It’s as if they kind of gave it their all in trying out this Great Society experiment and never got over the trauma of seeing the fascistic repression of the Nixon years play out. This is also a book that is very broadly about a repressive police state. I think it goes without saying that the police state we live in, even before the last two years, probably outdoes Pynchon’s worst imaginations when writing Brock Vond and his goons in Vineland.

    It’s clear that there’s a consensus feeling that the rightward backlash of the Nixon years were some sort of revenge against the hippies? Certainly this is what both Trump presidencies have felt like for us. But what was our Woodstock? I don’t even remember the years before 2016 even being very cool or transgressive. What are they even so angry about?
