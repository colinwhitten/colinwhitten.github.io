Been Down So Long It Looks Like Up To Me

Been Down So Long it Looks Like Up to Me, by Richard Farina

Gnossos Pappadapoulis’ central conflict is that he is desperately in search of something sincere and real, but reality has been so shitty and terrified him to the point of retreating into hedonism; he uses drugs and postures a detached coolness as a means by which he can extract himself from a grim and confusing reality. There’s a lot about this vague search for meaning through exemption while also pushing back against conservatism, somewhat. Hedonism as a sort of vague form of protest: where have we seen this before?

If you’re curious what the dialogue reads like a little, here is a tiny excerpt I really enjoy:

“Hey, Paps, cut that shit, will you? What’s wrong, you’ve really got Ouef’s clap? How’d you like some nice Mexican grass?”

“I got it all right; boy, do I have it. Right through the hole in the old Trojan, zippo-bang.”

What exactly am I supposed to do with this? This book, something of a cult classic, is probably most significant because of Farina’s proximity to Thomas Pynchon: they were friends at Cornell University and probably took classes from Vladimir Nabokov together. Thomas Pynchon notably dedicated Gravity’s Rainbow to Farina, and also notably wrote an introduction to this novel, an introduction which has become somewhat famous amongst fans of Pynchon for how honest and personally revealing he is in the essay. Farina died in a freak motorcycle accident in 1968, literally hours after this book, supposed to be his big break, was published. Can you believe that? I think mostly everyone reading this book today would be picking it up because they heard about Farina through Pynchon, and I’d say it’s maybe as close as you could get to Pynchon, without it being Pynchon himself! What that means: I don’t know! Fuck if I know! I still really don’t understand this shit very well at all, and I’m confused why I seem to still enjoy it. Is this really because I feel smart for reading it at all? Newsflash, it’s only smart or cool or impressive if you can even somewhat understand it!

Gnossos Pappadapoulis is way too horny and an asshole, but I think we can all relate to his struggle in coming of age and being confronted with a grim reality upon leaving college. On one hand, it’s very first world problems as he’s leaving a fictionalized version of Cornell University so ultimately who cares about him being let down by the world outside of college being not what he expected. With that said I think all of us experience some level of learning that all the virtues we were taught would take us far might actually stand in the way of us having nice and easy, comfortable lives. Being intellectually curious will not make you money. Nor will being compassionate, sensitive, or kind. Personally I think that the traits I’m most proud of in myself stand directly in the way of me having a good Lucrative Career. And any ways in which I’m able to get ahead are more from me leaning into traits I’d probably be less proud of!

Nobody cares if you read, or whatever. If you’re on some intellectual or creative journey, it almost certainly won’t make you any money nor help you make rent. It also won’t make you popular at parties. It might just make you mostly sad!
